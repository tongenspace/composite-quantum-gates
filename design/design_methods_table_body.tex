We write $B_r^{\rm n}$ for the square in $(\epsilon,\delta)$ and $B_r$ for the square in $(\epsilon,\delta_c)$, where $\delta_c=\Delta/\Omega_{\max}=\delta/p$. The threshold fraction $f_4$ counts samples with $\mathcal I\leq10^{-4}$; $f_4^{(0)}$ restricts it to the connected component containing the nominal point. In the shorter coverage searches, a smooth approximation to $f_4$ is maximized, rather than minimizing $J_w$:
\[

\] All averages and protection conditions are sampled; they do not certify continuum bounds.

Noise enters the candidate selection, rather than the deterministic optimization, for the edge-aware Hadamard rows of Table~\ref{table:design_methods}. The selection uses finite-band Gaussian $1/f$ detuning noise with $S_\xi(f)=A/f$, $A=3.6\times10^5\,{\rm Hz}^2$, on 1 Hz--1 MHz. The same fixed ensemble is used across candidates: 16 trajectories comprising eight independent antithetic pairs. The subsequent fixed-coefficient tests in the finite-edge appendix of the manuscript use fresh Gaussian coefficients, with separate ensembles for the domain comparison and the nominal bandwidth sweep. Within that sweep, the Gaussian coefficients are reused across cutoffs; the cutoff points are therefore correlated. H5c and the numerical $X$ controls are selected without dynamic noise. Exact meshes, smoothing parameters, bounds, starts and search settings are supplied in the repository.
